Road-Trip Day-Of Checks

A 15-minute walk-around you do the morning you leave — the morning that matters.

What this is

Long trips are the one time your car asks to be taken seriously. This is a short, kind checklist for departure morning: car, people, house, and paperwork. Nothing on it needs a mechanic.

How to use it

  1. Print once and keep inside a road-trip folder you reuse.
  2. The week before: do page 2 (oil due? tires aging? schedule now).
  3. Departure morning: work down page 3 in about 15 minutes.
  4. Tape the route + arrival ETAs on page 3 so someone at home always knows where you are.
Reminder: for a fully loaded car (roof box, 5 people, luggage), your manual's tire-pressure sticker lists a higher “full load” PSI. Use that, not the normal number.
Printing tips: print one copy per trip; write the trip name and dates at the top. Tired-brain tip: laminate or sleeve one copy so the same sheet works again and again.

Road-Trip Prep — The Week Before

Trip name: ______________________ Dates: ______________ Who's going: ______________________
Car
  • Book/confirm any due service (check your maintenance schedule printout first)
  • Tires: pressure (set to the sticker number), tread, and a serious look for slow leaks
  • Spare tire: holds air + jack and lug wrench present and you know where they are
  • Oil touching MAX? Coolant between lines? Brake reservoir at MAX?
  • All lights work (walk-around with a helper, 2 minutes)
  • Wipers still clean every pass? Wiper fluid full?
  • Scan for new noises, smells, or warning lights — fix small things now, not on I-80
House & logistics
  • Hold mail (USPS hold) or arrange pickup
  • Confirm pet/plant arrangements
  • Pay bills that come due while away
  • Download offline maps for the route
  • Confirm hotel + first-night booking
  • Tell one person your route + arrival day

Verify with your manual: if you're towing a trailer or small camper, read the manual's towing chapter — weight limits and mirror/brake requirements are model-specific, and they're real limits, not suggestions.

Departure Morning — 15 Minutes

Do it before the coffee's fully drunk. Two checks need a passenger; the rest take one person.
Around the car (walk clockwise)
  • Front tires look parked-correct, not saggy (just look — pressure was set this week)
  • Windshield: clean, and you've got working fluid to clear bugs
  • Lights on: headlights, brake lights (helper checks), turn signals, hazards
  • Under the car: no fresh puddles or drips this morning
  • Roof box / bike racks / trailer all strapped + locks engaged
  • Everything loose inside secured — no heavy stuff that can fly in a hard stop (do your manual's load rules: heavy items low and forward)
  • Glovebox stock-up: registration, insurance card, trusty info card
Inside the car
  • Phone mounts and chargers working
  • Seat + mirrors set for the first driver
  • Snacks + water within arm's reach
  • Kids'/pets' items buckled in
  • Music/podcasts queued (safety first: set before driving)
  • Medications, glasses, contacts packed in cabin (not trunk)
This-trip card: where are we, and when do we get there
Route via (main towns)
Expect arrival + date
Home contact who has this
Longest stretch without stops (plan water/rest every ~2 hrs)
Emergency-in-transit plan
  • Fuel/EV charge topped off the night before
  • Seats/suits/last bathroom before the door
  • Trash accounted for (bag + empty bottle plan)
  • One more look: everything's inside that's leaving with you

Verify with your manual: fully-loaded road trip = check that full-load tire pressure and any roof-load limits in the manual before you tie anything down.
