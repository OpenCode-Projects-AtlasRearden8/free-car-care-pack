Winter Car-Kit Checklist

A checklist for the bag or bin that lives in your trunk all winter — plus a quick winterizing to-do list.

What this is

Two pages: a build-the-kit shopping checklist, and a one-evening “get the car winter-ready” checklist. Build the kit once, then a quick glance each November is all it takes.

How to use it

  1. Page 2: check off what you already own, circle what to buy. The starred items are the “don't skip these” ones if you're on a budget.
  2. Put everything in one duffel or storage bin in the trunk.
  3. Page 3: work through the winterize list before the first freeze.
  4. Re-check the kit each fall — flashlight batteries go stale.
Reminder: tire chains, snow socks, or winter tires are model-specific — your manual's tire section lists what's approved and how to mount them. Trust the manual over big-box-store advice at the shelf.
Printing tips: print one copy now, and tape a second copy inside your winter storage bin lid. Milder climate? The starred basics still earn their trunk space.

Winter Car-Kit

Check ✔ when it's IN the kit. ★ = most important if you buy only a few things.
Safety & warmth
  • ★ Blanket, or a hooded emergency blanket
  • ★ Flashlight + fresh spare batteries
  • ★ First-aid kit (small store-bought one)
  • ★ Phone charger / 12V usb cable + power bank
  • Warm hat, gloves, spare socks (old ones are fine)
  • Hand warmers (2–3 pairs)
  • Whistle (cheap, loud, tiny)
  • Water bottle or pouches (refill each fall)
  • Snacks that survive cold (granola bars, nuts)
  • Any daily medications you couldn't skip for a night
Getting unstuck & seen
  • ★ Ice scraper / snow brush
  • ★ Jumper cables or jump pack (charged)
  • ★ Folding shovel, or any old small spade
  • ★ Traction help: sand, cat litter, or traction mats
  • Reflective triangles or LED flares (3)
  • Tow strap (works only from the manual's proper tie points)
  • Bright spare scarf or vest to wear in the dark
  • Small tool kit / multi-tool
  • Duct tape, a few zip ties
  • Windshield de-icer spray

Verify with your manual: your manual's “Emergency” or “Winter driving” chapter often has a factory list of what your battery, cloths, and exact jack setup can and can't handle — worth two minutes before you shop.

Get Winter-Ready — One Evening

Do everything on page 1, then knock this out one evening per year. Most items are free.
  • Wiper blades: replace if they smear (they're cheap and they die every 1–2 winters)
  • Washer fluid: fill with winter / de-icer formula (the blue summer bug wash freezes)
  • Tires: check pressure cold — it drops about 1 PSI per 10°F of temperature drop
  • Tires: check tread with the quarter test (head up; if Washington's head is covered, you're OK)
  • Battery: 3+ years old? Have it tested free at most parts stores before a cold snap
  • Oil / fluids: schedule a service if one is due in the next month
  • Headlights / brake lights: have someone watch while you flip each one
  • Scrape the inside windows once — they fog worse than you think
  • Clear snow off the roof too, not just the windshield (it slides onto yours at speed)
  • Leave the fuel level above 1/4 tank (keeps fuel lines and a warm cabin safer)
  • Test defrosters front AND rear
  • Put the kit from page 2 in the trunk (where it will actually live this year)
  • Make sure the spare tire still holds air (and you remember where it hides)
